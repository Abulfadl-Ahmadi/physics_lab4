# Experiment 9: Ionization of Gases

This directory contains the experimental data and the final report for the **Ionization of Gases** experiment.

## Overview

This experiment investigates the ionization of gas atoms by electron impact, measuring the ionization potential of mercury vapor. By accelerating electrons through a potential difference and observing at which voltage ionization current begins to flow, the binding energy of the outermost electron in the mercury atom can be determined.

## Theoretical Background

### Atomic Ionization

When an electron with sufficient kinetic energy collides with a neutral atom, it can knock out one of the atom's bound electrons, creating a free electron-ion pair. The minimum energy required to remove the most loosely bound electron from a neutral atom in its ground state is called the **ionization potential** (or ionization energy).

For mercury (Hg), the ionization energy is approximately 10.44 eV, corresponding to the removal of one of the 6s electrons from the closed-shell ground state configuration [Xe] 4f¹⁴ 5d¹⁰ 6s².

### Franck-Hertz Connection

This experiment is closely related to the Franck-Hertz experiment. While the Franck-Hertz experiment observes the first *excitation* energy (4.9 eV for Hg, corresponding to the ¹S₀ → ³P₁ transition), the ionization experiment observes the energy required to completely remove an electron. The ratio of ionization to excitation energy provides information about the atomic energy level structure.

### Experimental Method

Electrons are emitted from a heated cathode and accelerated toward a grid. In the space between the grid and the collector, they encounter mercury vapor. When the accelerating voltage exceeds the ionization potential, inelastic collisions ionize mercury atoms, producing additional free electrons that are collected as measurable ionization current.
